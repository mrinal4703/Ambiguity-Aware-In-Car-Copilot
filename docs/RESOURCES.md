## Project papers (from the problem statement)
- Kirmayr et al. (2026), CAR-bench: evaluating consistency and limit-awareness of LLM agents (environment, evaluation, Pass@3 vs Pass^3)
- Du et al. (2024), Towards Proactive Interactions for In-Vehicle Conversational Assistants Utilizing LLMs (Rewrite + ReAct + Reflect)
- Suri et al. (2026), Structured Uncertainty Guided Clarification for LLM Agents (EVPI-based question selection)

## Textbooks (by module)
- Russell and Norvig, *Artificial Intelligence: A Modern Approach*: search (modules 5), constraint satisfaction (6), automated planning (7), making simple decisions incl. the value of information (4), making complex decisions / MDPs and reinforcement learning (8)
- Sutton and Barto, *Reinforcement Learning: An Introduction* (free online): tabular Q-learning (8)

## Module 4 specifically
- AIMA chapter on making simple decisions, section on the value of information: the textbook definition of EVPI
- R. A. Howard, "Information Value Theory" (1966): the classic reference
- Alternative question-selection criterion to compare against: expected information gain (entropy reduction)
- Calibration of model confidence (module 3 feeds module 4, so this matters): Guo et al. (2017), "On Calibration of Modern Neural Networks"; Tian et al. (2023), "Just Ask for Calibration" (verbalized confidence from LLMs)
